Tuesday 28 July 2026
Palermo — Arab-Norman Splendour
🏨 Palermo 🏖️ Mondello lido 🚗 ~35 min driving
▾
Morning

Mondello lido — Palermo's famous organised beach, 12 km from centre. Fine white sand, retro Art Nouveau pavilion, turquoise Tyrrhenian water. Rent umbrella + 2 sunbeds (€20–30/day). Relax until noon.

Afternoon

Cappella Palatina inside the Norman Palace — 6,340 sqm of Byzantine gold mosaics, the most breathtaking interior in Sicily. Visit early afternoon before it gets crowded. Then La Cuba (12th-century Arab-Norman pavilion) → Piazza della Vergogna (Piazza Pretoria — the nude fountain that scandalised the church) → La Martorana church → Cimitero dei Cappuccini — the famous catacombs with ~8,000 mummified bodies in robes. Eerie and extraordinary.

Evening

Lunch option: Ciuri Ciuri (Via Vittorio Emanuele 188, rated 4.9/5) — the first all-you-can-eat Sicilian restaurant in Palermo. Try 8–10 traditional dishes in one sitting: pasta alla Norma, caponata, arancine, cannoli. Available Mon–Fri, perfect for your Tuesday. Evening dinner near Teatro Massimo — Bisso Bistrot (inside a bookshop) for pasta con le sarde, or Trattoria Del Massimo for traditional home cooking.

🇮🇹 Local Insider — Palermo Details
Start the city visit from the port area — best parking. Walk to Porta Felice first. Optional: Monreale Cathedral can be added this afternoon (20 min drive) if you have energy — but we have it properly on Day 3 return from Cefalù. For lunch: Ciuri Ciuri (Via Vittorio Emanuele 188) — Sicily's first all-you-can-eat traditional Sicilian restaurant, rated 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor and recommended by our Italian friend. Mon–Fri only, perfect for Tuesday.

Cefalù & Monreale
🏨 Palermo 🏖️ Cefalù lido 🚗 ~130 min driving
▾
Morning

Drive east to Cefalù (1 hr from Palermo). Visit the Cefalù Cathedral (1131 AD) first — magnificent Arab-Norman architecture with glittering Byzantine Christ Pantocrator mosaic. Then the hidden Lavatoio Medievale — ancient wash-house fed by natural springs below street level. Walk the tiny medieval centro storico.

Evening

Drive back toward Palermo stopping at Monreale Cathedral — the world's greatest Norman cathedral, with 6,340 sqm of Byzantine gold mosaics. Closes 5:30pm — time the return accordingly. Dinner back in Palermo.

🇮🇹 Local Insider — Cefalù Dining

Greek!
Thursday 30 July 2026
Segesta, Scopello & Tonnara di Scopello
🏨 Trapani 🏖️ Tonnara di Scopello 🚗 ~120 min driving
▾
Morning

Checkout Palermo, drive toward Trapani. Stop at Segesta (1 hr from Palermo) — one of the most hauntingly beautiful Greek temples in existence, rising alone from wild hills. Built 430–420 BC by the Elymians. The unfinished theatre above has extraordinary views. Allow 2 hrs. Go early — fully exposed site, heat builds fast.

Afternoon

Continue to Scopello village — tiny hamlet with an old tonnara (tuna fishery) and dramatic faraglioni rock stacks. Then Tonnara di Scopello — one of the most iconic swimming spots in western Sicily. Ancient tuna fishery buildings on a rocky promontory, enormous faraglioni rising from turquoise water. Entry fee ~€10pp. Arrive before 2pm — admission limited. Rocky entry but extraordinary water.

Evening

Check into Trapani base. Dinner at La Bettolaccia — book ahead. The definitive couscous al pesce (fish couscous) — the North African-influenced dish unique to the Trapani area. Via Fardella 23, Trapani.

🏛️ Greek Connection — Segesta
The Elymians built this temple to impress Greek visitors but were never conquered by Greece. The sophisticated Greek architectural form adopted by non-Greeks is a remarkable statement of cultural power. As a Greek you'll feel this history viscerally.

Drive north to San Vito Lo Capo (45 min from Trapani). 3 km of white powder sand, Blue Flag, shallow and calm Mediterranean water. THE organised lido of western Sicily — multiple beach clubs, umbrellas, sunbeds, bars. Arrive early (9am) to secure the best spot. Spend the full morning.

If you prefer a wilder experience, Cala dell'Uzzo inside the Zingaro Reserve is 20 min from San Vito. Extraordinary cove, protected reserve water, pristine. Requires a 20-min hike in — bring water and food, no facilities. Decide on the day based on energy levels.

Lido Signorino, Marsala & Mazara del Vallo
🏨 Trapani 🏖️ Lido Signorino 🚗 ~115 min driving
▾
Morning

Drive south to Lido Signorino (40 min from Trapani) — organised lido between Trapani and Marsala. Long sandy beach, calm shallow water, Blue Flag. Full facilities, sunbeds, beach bar. Quieter than San Vito on a Sunday. Swim and relax until noon.

Afternoon

10 min drive to Marsala: Museo Baglio Anselmi (Punic warship museum — the only intact Carthaginian war galley ever found, 3rd century BC, 45 min) → salt pans and windmills (stunning landscape, pink flamingos, beautiful in afternoon light, 20 min walk). Then continue to Mazara del Vallo (25 min) — walk the extraordinary Arab quarter medina, more North African than Italian. Lunch/late lunch at L'Antica Sicilia for gambero rosso di Mazara — the world-famous red prawn, eaten here at the source.

Evening

Return to Trapani (40 min). Last evening in the west — aperitivo on the waterfront and cous cous con pesce leftovers or light dinner. Tomorrow is the big transit day.

🇮🇹 Local Insider — Mazara del Vallo
Your Italian friend rates this as the best fish in Italy. The Tunisian fishing community settled here for generations makes the medina feel genuinely North African — unlike anywhere else on the island. The gambero rosso prawns are world-renowned. Order them raw (crudo) if available — the real experience.

Monday 3 August 2026
Selinunte, Scala dei Turchi & Valle dei Templi
🏨 Syracuse/Ortigia 🚗 ~170 min driving
▾
Morning

Checkout Trapani by 8:00am — this is essential. Drive to Selinunte Archaeological Park (1.5 hrs). Arrive at opening (8:30am — before the heat). The largest Greek archaeological site in the world by area — seven massive temples on a coastal promontory. Founded 628 BC, destroyed by Carthage 409 BC. Allow 2 hrs. No swim today — keep moving.

Afternoon

Drive to Scala dei Turchi (1 hr from Selinunte). The iconic white limestone cliff sculpted by wind and sea into natural steps above azure water. Walk to the viewpoint, take photos, 30–45 min stop. The colour of the water and the sculpted white rock are genuinely surreal — unlike anything else in Europe. No swim — just the spectacle. Then 20 min to Agrigento old town — walk the Arab quarter streets, find an aperitivo bar, rest for 45 min.

Evening

At 4:30pm sharp — Valle dei Templi. The finest collection of Doric temples outside Greece. Fully exposed south-facing ridge — visiting at this hour means heat has broken, golden light, open until dusk (~7:30–8pm). The Temple of Concordia (5th c. BC) is better preserved than most temples in Greece itself. Allow 2–2.5 hrs. Then drive to Syracuse/Ortigia (1 hr 20 min). Check in. Late dinner on Ortigia island.

☀️ Heat Warning — Valle dei Templi
Never visit in the morning. Fully exposed ridge, zero shade, 40°C+ in July/August. The 4:30pm golden hour visit is the correct and only comfortable approach. Agrigento locals do not visit before 5pm in summer.
🏛️ Greek Connection — Selinunte
Founded by Greeks from Megara Hyblaea in 628 BC. Destroyed by Carthage in 409 BC in one of antiquity's most brutal sackings — the ruins were never rebuilt. What you see is essentially a frozen moment of destruction from 2,400 years ago.

Tuesday 4 August 2026
Ortigia, Neapolis & Pantalica
🏨 La Casa di Marta — Ortigia 🏖️ Fontane Bianche (optional) 🚗 ~110 min driving
▾
Morning

Start on foot from your hotel — Ortigia island morning walk. The original Greek colony founded 734 BC by Corinthians. Piazza del Duomo at 9am before tourist groups arrive — the Baroque Duomo was built directly around the Temple of Athena (5th c. BC), original Doric columns still visible and touchable inside the church walls. Mind-bending. Fonte Aretusa — the freshwater spring that Pindar and Cicero wrote about, papyrus plants, ducks, mythological atmosphere.

Late Morning

Drive 15 min to Neapolis Archaeological Park. The Greek Theatre (5th c. BC, still used for performances today) → the Ear of Dionysius (enormous limestone cave with extraordinary acoustics, legend says Dionysius listened to prisoners' whispers from above) → Roman amphitheatre. Allow 1.5–2 hrs.

Afternoon

Lunch on Ortigia, then drive 40 min to Pantalica — UNESCO site, a dramatic limestone gorge with over 5,000 prehistoric rock-cut tombs carved into the cliff faces by the Sicels (pre-Greek indigenous Sicilians) between the 13th and 7th centuries BC. Access via Sortino — drive up to the plateau, park, walk among the upper tombs and visit the Anaktoron (Bronze Age palace ruin). Extraordinary views over the honeycomb of tombs in the cliffs below. No serious hiking required — the plateau is accessible by car. Allow 1.5–2 hrs. Optional: descend into the gorge for the Byzantine village and river swim (30–45 min hike each way).

Evening

Return to Ortigia for dinner — A Putia (no reservations, arrive at 8pm) for pistachio pasta and pasta alla Norma. Or Il Tiranno with Forum views for a special dinner. The Ortigia evening passeggiata along Lungomare di Levante is essential — golden light over the sea, locals strolling, perfect aperitivo atmosphere.

🏛️ Greek Connection — Ortigia & Neapolis
Syracuse at its peak was the most powerful Greek city in the world — it defeated Athens' great military expedition of 415 BC. The theatre here still stages Aeschylus and Sophocles in Greek every summer (INDA Festival, May–July). Walking the same stones as Archimedes, Pindar and Cicero is a profound experience.
🌊 Optional Alternative — Fontane Bianche
If you'd prefer a beach afternoon instead of Pantalica: Fontane Bianche lido is 20 min south of Ortigia — white sand, clear water, well organised. A relaxed beach afternoon is a valid alternative on a hot August day. Decide based on energy and preference.

Taormina, Savoca, Castelmola → Catania
🏨 Catania — Le Suites Del Duomo 🏖️ Isola Bella (optional) 🚗 ~140 min driving
▾
Morning

Checkout La Casa di Marta in Ortigia. Drive directly to Taormina (1hr 20min). Greek-Roman Theatre — 3rd century BC, rebuilt by Romans, Etna smoking behind the stage, Ionian Sea glittering below. The most dramatic archaeological site in Sicily — open until 7pm. Walk Corso Umberto and the terraced Villa Comunale gardens.

Afternoon

Savoca (20 min from Taormina) — The Godfather village, Bar Vitelli where Michael Corleone met Apollonia, almost unchanged since 1971. Castelmola (10 min above Taormina) — ruined castle, panoramic views to Etna and Calabria, vino di mandorla at Bar Turrisi. Optional swim: Isola Bella cable car for an hour at the iconic beach below Taormina.

Evening

Drive to Catania (45 min from Taormina) via the A18 motorway. Check into Le Suites Del Duomo — literally on Piazza del Duomo, 5km from the airport. Evening walk: Piazza del Duomo, Fontana dell'Elefante (Catania's symbol), Via Etnea. Dinner in Catania — try Osteria Antica Marina for seafood (1 min from hotel) or A Putia dell'Ostello. Catania's evening atmosphere is one of the liveliest in Sicily.

🎭 Taormina Arte Festival
World-class concerts and opera performed in the ancient Greek theatre all summer — artists have included Bocelli, Sting, major orchestras. Check taormina-arte.com for programme on your date. If a concert lands on your night, consider sleeping in Taormina instead — but be aware you'd need a late drive to Catania the morning of your flight.
🛏️ Why Catania for the Last Night
Le Suites Del Duomo sits literally on Piazza del Duomo, 5km from CTA airport. Day 12 morning becomes completely stress-free — no early drive from Taormina, no last-minute panic. Just walk to your car, 10-min drive to airport. Worth giving up the romance of waking up in Taormina for the practical peace of mind.

Eat & Drink

What You Must Try

🍧
Granita con BriocheThe Sicilian breakfast. Frozen fruit slush in a soft brioche bun. Try pistachio in Taormina, lemon in Catania, almond in Noto, mulberry in Cefalù. Every morning.
🍪
Cannolo SicilianoCrispy fried tube filled to order with fresh ricotta. NEVER pre-filled. If the shell looks soft, walk away. Ask for it filled in front of you.
🍚
Arancine (Palermo) / Arancini (East)Fried rice balls filled with ragù, mozzarella and peas. The great street food debate of Sicily. Buy from a rosticceria, never a tourist restaurant.
🍝
Pasta con le SardePasta with fresh sardines, wild fennel, pine nuts, raisins and saffron. The definitive Palermo dish — Arab-influenced, gutsy and complex. Bisso Bistrot, Palermo.
🍆
Pasta alla NormaPasta with fried aubergine, tomato, ricotta salata and basil. Named after Bellini's opera, invented in Catania. The great vegetarian Sicilian dish.
🥣
Couscous al PesceFish couscous unique to the Trapani area — North African import. Complex broth, saffron-spiced. La Bettolaccia in Trapani is the definitive version.

Magna Graecia

Sicily's Greek Heritage

Sicily was the heart of Magna Graecia. More Greeks lived here than in Greece itself at the height of the 5th century BC. These sites will feel like a homecoming.

Valley of the Temples
Agrigento — ancient Akragas · Day 8

Founded 582 BC by Rhodians and Geloans. Pindar called it "the most beautiful city of mortals." The Temple of Concordia is one of the best-preserved Doric temples in the world — better than most in Greece. Visit at golden hour 4:30pm.

UNESCO · Must Visit · Golden Hour Only
Neapolis & Ortigia — Syracuse
Ancient Syracusae · Day 9

Founded 734 BC by Corinthians. The most powerful Greek city in the world at its peak — it defeated Athens' great expedition of 415 BC. The Greek Theatre still stages Aeschylus and Sophocles in summer. The Duomo was literally built inside the Temple of Athena.

Theatre Still In Use · Temple Inside Church
Segesta Temple & Theatre
Calatafimi-Segesta · Day 4

Built 430–420 BC by the Elymians — indigenous Sicilians who adopted Greek architectural forms without being conquered by Greece. Unfinished, alone in wild hills. More mysterious and haunting than any ruin in Greece itself.

Most Atmospheric · Non-Greek Builders
Selinunte Archaeological Park
Ancient Selinus · Day 8

Founded 628 BC. The largest Greek archaeological site in the world by area. Seven temples across multiple hills. Destroyed 409 BC by Carthage in one of antiquity's most brutal sackings — the ruins are frozen at that moment of destruction.

World's Largest Site · Early Morning Only
Greek-Roman Theatre — Taormina
Ancient Tauromenion · Day 11

Originally built 3rd century BC by Greeks, later rebuilt by Romans. Still used for live performances. The most photographed ancient monument in Sicily — Etna smouldering behind the stage, Ionian Sea below. Afternoon light is exceptional.

Still In Use · Taormina Arte Festival
Cappella Palatina & Monreale
Palermo · Days 2 & 3

The Norman kings brought Greek Byzantine craftsmen from Constantinople to create these extraordinary gold mosaic programmes. The artistic tradition is direct Greek Orthodox inheritance — the same iconography, gold grounds and style as Byzantine churches in Greece, but preserved in completeness never seen elsewhere.

Liberty Lines — book tickets in advance online for July/August. First boat ~7am, last return ~20:30. Buy a day return. Rent bikes at the port on arrival (€10–15/day). Island is flat and perfectly bikeable in 30 min.

Limited daily admissions — arrive before 2pm. Entry ~€10pp. Rocky entry to water — water shoes useful. No sand, but the setting is extraordinary. Village of Scopello above has a small bar for post-swim coffee.
